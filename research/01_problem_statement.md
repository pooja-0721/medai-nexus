Healthcare information is generated in many different forms, including medical images such as X-rays, MRI and CT scans, physiological signals such as ECGs, laboratory and blood reports, and textual descriptions of symptoms. Existing artificial intelligence systems are often developed for a specific medical modality or a narrowly defined clinical task. This fragmentation can make AI-assisted healthcare technologies difficult to access and use through a single, understandable interface, particularly for users without medical or technical expertise.

At the same time, many AI-based medical analysis systems focus primarily on prediction accuracy while providing limited explanations that help users understand the basis and limitations of the model's output. The lack of an accessible interface connecting different types of medical information also limits the potential of AI to provide unified, user-oriented decision support.

Therefore, this project proposes the development of MedAI Nexus, a multimodal AI-assisted healthcare platform designed to process and analyze selected forms of medical information, including X-rays, medical images, blood reports, ECG signals, brain scans, and user-provided symptoms. The platform will integrate specialized machine learning, deep learning, computer vision, signal-processing, and natural language processing techniques through a unified interface.

The system will also incorporate an AI-powered conversational assistant to help users understand analysis results and medical terminology using clear and accessible language. Explainability techniques will be investigated to make model outputs more interpretable, while privacy, responsible AI, and appropriate safety limitations will be considered throughout the system design.

The proposed platform is intended as an AI-assisted research and decision-support system rather than a replacement for qualified healthcare professionals or a tool for independent medical diagnosis. The project will evaluate individual models and the overall framework using appropriate performance, interpretability, and usability measures.
